# Literature review output format

Use this structure for a full review. Every factual sentence cites a source that a Corbis tool returned in this conversation or that the user supplied.

## 1. Question and scope

The question, field, time window, journal or year constraints, and the date of the search.

## 2. Summary

Three to five sentences on what the evidence says and how strong it is.

## 3. Themes

For each theme: the finding, the papers that support it (author-year), and the strength of the evidence (strong, moderate, or limited), with a short reason for that label.

## 4. Agreement and disagreement

Where papers agree, where they conflict, and plausible reasons for conflict (sample, period, market, method).

## 5. Gaps and open questions

Gaps visible in the returned papers. Label any gap you infer beyond them as your own judgment.

## 6. Evidence table

| Paper (author, year) | Setting and data | Method | Main finding | Relevance to the question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 7. Limits of this review

What the search could miss: fields or journals outside coverage, recent working papers, non-English work, and anything the tools flagged as a coverage warning.

## 8. Sources

Full references exactly as returned (authors, year, title, journal, DOI or link). Do not add fields the tools did not return.
