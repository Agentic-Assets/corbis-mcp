# Allocation decision framework

Use these inspection questions for the relevant decision. Do not treat them as evidence that a particular strategy outperforms.

## Target versus implementation

Track policy target weight, funded NAV, unfunded commitments, and cash-call timing as distinct quantities. A target change specifies an intended weight, not a callable obligation. Assess incremental calls from supplied or verified commitment terms, call schedules, or an implementation plan, including changes to existing commitments. A difference in target percentage points supplies neither amount nor timing of calls. If a stress assumption does not specify whether calls concern existing commitments or a proposed allocation, keep that attribution unknown.

## Compare implementable alternatives

| Decision | Inspect before drawing the implication |
|---|---|
| Private versus public assets | Net fees and carried interest, leverage, access, commitment pacing, vintage, valuation frequency, stale or smoothed marks, capital calls, redemption limits, selection and survivorship biases. Distinguish diversification inferred from marks from realizable liquidity. |
| Active versus passive | Appropriate investable benchmark, net fees, trading costs, taxes where relevant, factor exposure, style drift, capacity, manager selection and persistence. Separate aggregate or historical results from evidence about a particular available manager. |
| Factor allocation | Factor definition, construction and turnover, benchmark overlap, gross versus net returns, data mining and multiple testing, out-of-sample evidence, shorting and financing assumptions, crowding and investor capacity. Do not label a backtest an implementable live track record. |
| Cross-asset diversification | Comparable currency and return frequencies, lookback window, valuation method, unstable correlations, stress-period coverage, common economic exposures, tail or concentration risk. Do not infer risk contribution from portfolio weights alone. |
| Rebalancing and implementation | Target policy versus drift, bands or calendar rules, cash flows, transaction costs, taxes, liquidity and governance approvals. Separate rebalancing to policy from an unsupported tactical forecast. |

Check whether reported returns are nominal or real, gross or net, levered or unlevered, and time-weighted or cash-flow-weighted. Preserve units and horizons. Identify liabilities, spending obligations, currency exposure, and governance constraints when these could reverse the implication. Mark missing information instead of manufacturing comparable estimates. Do not calculate a return spread, alpha, upper bound, or break-even fee by subtracting incompatible metrics, such as pooled gross IRR and net time-weighted return.

## Source ledger before synthesis

For a Full brief, show one compact row per source before findings:

| Record | Provenance and verification | Actual inspection depth |
|---|---|---|
| User's record ID or exact retrieved citation | User-supplied (synthetic if stated), or Corbis-retrieved; do not call supplied records Corbis-verified | Metadata, abstract, excerpt, or full text actually available |

Use a source sentence with the same information in a Quick answer. Map factual findings and all numerical inputs back to this ledger or the user's explicit inputs.

## Evidence table

| Source and inspection depth | Sample, period, investor or asset universe | Finding and comparison basis | Limitations or counterevidence | Conditional portfolio implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact citation; abstract, excerpt, or full text | Preserve the source's population | Include benchmark, costs and measurement horizon when known | Identify missing data, possible methodological risks or non-transferable conditions | Label analyst interpretation and required investor assumptions |

Report what an abstract actually establishes and which checks need full-text inspection. Do not attach precision, significance, or causal certainty absent from the available text.

## Full brief structure

1. **Source ledger, then decision:** establish provenance and actual inspection depth before findings; state the investor's choice, scope, evidence date, and conditional takeaway. An unsupported target does not establish a lower, staged, or pilot target.
2. **Evidence:** provide the table, strongest contrary result, and whether the disagreement reflects different samples or definitions.
3. **Implementation comparison:** assess costs, exposures, liquidity, commitments, governance, and constraints relevant to feasible alternatives.
4. **Allocation considerations:** explain which conditions favor each alternative. Identify inputs needed before weights or actions can be assessed; present no invented optimizer output.
5. **Next decision and monitoring:** give a few diligence questions, evidence that could reverse the view, and measurable monitoring criteria where available.
6. **Sources:** list only retrieved or supplied sources with exact available links or identifiers.

## Example decision translations

- **"Should our endowment increase private assets from 20% to 35%?"** Review the rationale and contrary evidence, then check existing exposures, spending, unfunded commitments, liquidity and governance. Do not endorse the proposed weight from general research alone. For requested cash-flow stress, calculate only from supplied or verified spending, capital calls, realizable liquidity and timing. For a requested cash bridge, subtract spending and capital calls from available cash, adding distributions or other funding only when supplied; do not add a new NAV path or assume calls increase asset values. Keep target arithmetic separate from any implementation-dependent new commitments or calls. Show target-dollar arithmetic only under an explicitly requested fixed-NAV assumption, and label it policy arithmetic rather than a funding plan. Identify missing valuation, distributions and timing inputs without supplying them.
- **"Is active management worth its fee?"** Compare net results against an appropriate benchmark and exposures; distinguish broad evidence from manager-specific selection claims. Insufficient support for a proposed weight is not evidence for a smaller pilot allocation.
- **"Would adding value and momentum diversify our stock-bond portfolio?"** Inspect construction, overlap, costs and out-of-sample evidence. Require actual exposure and covariance inputs before reporting numerical portfolio risk effects.
