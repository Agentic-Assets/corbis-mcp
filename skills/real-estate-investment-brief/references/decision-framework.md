# Real estate decision framework

Apply the rows relevant to the decision. Use them as inspection questions, not claims that any effect exists in a particular dataset.

## Compare like with like

| Decision | Inspect before drawing the implication |
|---|---|
| Core versus noncore | Source definitions of each strategy; stabilized income versus lease-up, redevelopment, or development; property quality and concentration; strategy drift; financing and manager selection. Avoid treating a label as a comparable risk bucket. |
| Geographic or sector diversification | Actual geographic and sector exposures, economic drivers, portfolio concentration, currency and hedging, common observation periods, and evidence from stress periods. Separate a diversification hypothesis from a measured portfolio benefit. |
| Listed versus private real estate | Underlying asset exposure, leverage, fees, liquidity, valuation frequency, return aggregation, and benchmark construction. Check whether different return intervals or appraisal smoothing affect comparisons. |
| Leverage and liquidity | Asset-level versus fund-level borrowing, recourse, refinancing and covenant exposure, redemption or lockup terms, capital calls, and ability to meet cash needs. Do not equate reported volatility with all investment risk. |
| Fund or manager selection | Vintage, geography, sector, cash-flow timing, gross versus net returns, IRR versus time-weighted returns, selection and survivorship biases, and whether returns include unrealized marks. Do not compare incompatible performance metrics as rankings. |

For every material numerical finding, preserve its units, measurement horizon, uncertainty, and the source's comparison basis. Report when adjustments or robustness checks were not observable. Never invent an adjustment to make studies comparable. For example, 12% gross pooled IRR and 6% net time-weighted return cannot establish a 6 percentage point spread, net upper bound, or fee break-even. Report both with their definitions; identify data needed for a comparable calculation.

## Source ledger before synthesis

For a Full brief, show one compact row per source before findings:

| Record | Provenance and verification | Actual inspection depth |
|---|---|---|
| User's record ID or exact retrieved citation | User-supplied (synthetic if stated), or Corbis-retrieved; do not call supplied records Corbis-verified | Metadata, abstract, excerpt, or full text actually available |

Keep the ledger separate from the finding table when different records have different inspection depths. In a Quick answer, give the same information in a source sentence.

## Evidence table

Build a compact table with one row per decision-relevant finding:

| Source and inspection depth | Sample, period, market and vehicle | Finding with return/risk basis | Limitation or contrary evidence | Implication and applicability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact citation; abstract, excerpt, or full text | Include known geography, sector and strategy | Preserve net/gross, leverage and metric qualifiers | State missing methods, possible selection issues or conflicting results | Label your interpretation; state the condition under which it applies |

Keep the evidence trail visible when an abstract is all that is available. Use language such as "the abstract reports" and avoid numerical or methodological detail absent from it.

## Full brief structure

1. **Source ledger, then decision:** establish provenance and actual inspection depth before findings; state alternatives, scope, evidence date, and conditional takeaway. Missing evidence for one option does not establish a preference for the other.
2. **Evidence:** include the table, any disagreement, and the strength of the support for each major conclusion.
3. **Investment implications:** compare income, growth, downside, liquidity, leverage, capital calls, implementation capacity, and concentration where relevant. Separate evidence from interpretation; do not infer steady income, observed volatility, or a leverage explanation from strategy labels alone.
4. **Diligence before action:** name the few facts that could change the assessment, who should obtain them, and why they matter. Treat requests to a manager as draft questions, not instructions to contact them.
5. **Sources:** list only retrieved or supplied sources with links or identifiers exactly as available.

## Example decision translations

- **"What does research say about core versus noncore, and how much should we hold?"** Compare definitions and net performance evidence first. If neither record measures income, say the income-based preference is unresolved. Do not favor core from its label or endorse less noncore simply because a proposed larger weight is unsupported. Identify missing liquidity, commitments, leverage limits, objectives and existing exposures before portfolio-specific percentages.
- **"Should our pension add private real estate or REITs?"** Compare underlying exposure and supplied liquidity terms; ask for missing redemption, lockup and distribution terms instead of inserting usual vehicle practices. Flag valuation and horizon mismatches. If comparing real estate to bonds or equities, route the broader allocation to `investment-allocation-brief`.
- **"Does geographic diversification protect an industrial portfolio?"** Preserve the requested property sector and market scope, check what the sources measure, and distinguish proposed protection from observed stress-period results.
